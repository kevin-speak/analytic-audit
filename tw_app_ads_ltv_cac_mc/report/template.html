Do creative signals predict LTV/CAC? A Monte Carlo read of 243 app ads

Each ad's LTV/CAC is simulated times from its SSOT trial starts, an uncertain trial-to-paid rate, Meta-observed purchases, and the plan mix of its trialists. The same draws re-score the Scalability Predictor (SP), installs per mille (IPM) and trials per mille impressions (TPI), so every correlation and every winner-vs-rest lift below carries a 90% interval instead of a point.

6 · Is SP ≥ 2 the right bar?

Does the SP ≥ 2 gate actually predict LTV/CAC?

Three tests. First, sweep the SP threshold and watch the LTV/CAC lift of ads passing the P1 gate (SP ≥ t and ≥ 10 installs). Second, split the P1 gate into its two conditions to see which one carries the lift. Third, benchmark the gate against simpler alternatives on lift, hit rate (share of gated ads that clear 1×) and AUC (how well the raw score ranks ads that clear 1× above those that don't; 0.5 = coin flip).

Simulation stability

4,000 vs 10,000 draws

More draws only shrink the simulation's own sampling noise. The intervals below are set by the data (how many paid users each ad is credited with, the trial-to-paid rate, the plan mix), so they do not narrow with more draws. Once the summaries stop moving, extra draws add nothing.

7 · Proposed formula

SP2 = Efficiency Index × Scale Index

A score that reads from installs, which arrive within hours, instead of trials, which take days and SKAN postbacks. It encodes the two things the current SP does not: whether the ad is projected to hit the 0.8× LTV/CAC target, and whether it can actually win delivery.

8 · Backtest

SP2 against the 10-install rule, replayed day by day

Every ad's daily Meta data is replayed to answer three questions: does SP2 give a verdict sooner than waiting for 10 installs; on the same 10-install date, which score better captures the ads that end at or above 0.85× LTV/CAC; and how the two rank the scaling campaign. The current SP is recomputed with the Option B placement formula on the data available that day.
